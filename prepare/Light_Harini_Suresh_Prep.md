## Paper summary 
A Framework for Understanding Sources of Harm throughout the Machine Learning Life Cycle
This is a very interesting paper because many of the research papers we read as professionals in computer science are very pro-AI, while this article breaks down how the ML cycle could be harmful to society. I agree with the main point of this paper that bias of these models first starts in the development phase of the technology. So many software engineers are socially unaware of several major societary issues and they design their AI models to poorly approach disparities. Computer science is still only viewed as a scientific field but it has rapidly become more involved in social issues. This presents a major problem today because computer scientists still are not receiving ethnics training and therefore, do not have the proper skills or knowledge to effectively make ethical AI systems

This article talks about two different types of harm these models can cause in society; allocative harm and representation harm. Allocative harm is present in models where they need to choose a subset of a pool of people for processes like loan acceptance and healthcare triage.A loan approval system trained on historical data may learn patterns shaped by past discrimination. Representational harm happens when models use biased data or data that underrepresents minority groups. This underrepresentation causes unequal treatment, exacerbates stereotypes, and reinforces biases. Allocative harm is easier to detect and therefore easier to look for strategies to prevent, but representational harm is harder to definitely identify when it is occurring despite the seriousness of the issue. Allocative harm can severely damage society before anyone can catch on to what is occurring. This could have long lasting, negative effects on society that can be very challenging to reverse.
